How the ranking works

Last updated 18 September 2026

SnusBattle does not make objective quality assessments of products. The ranking shows what users have chosen when two products were placed side by side. It is not a health recommendation and says nothing about how products compare in terms of risk.

The basic idea

You see two products. You pick one. The winner stays and faces the next randomly selected product. Each such comparison is called a battle.

The calculation

The ranking uses Elo, the same system used to rank chess players. Every product starts at 1500.

Before each battle an expected result is calculated from the difference between the two ratings:

expected = 1 / (1 + 10^((opponent rating − own rating) / 400))

After the battle both ratings are adjusted:

new rating = old rating ± 32 × (1 − expected)

The winner gains, the loser loses the same amount. The factor 32 determines how fast ratings move.

What this means in practice

SituationEffect
A favourite beats a low-rated productSmall change — the result was expected
A low-rated product beats the favouriteLarge change — the result was unexpected
Two evenly matched products meetAround 16 points change hands

Number of battles matters

A product with few battles has an uncertain rating. The 30-day rankings therefore require at least five battles before a product appears. Battle counts are shown in the lists so you can judge how much weight a figure deserves.

The ranking changes

The more people vote, the more the lists move. A position is a snapshot, not a final result.

Manipulation

Each browser receives a randomly generated id that limits votes per minute. The protection is simple and can be circumvented by anyone determined to do so. We show battle counts openly so you can judge the basis yourself.

Product information versus votes

Nicotine content, flavour and format are product data and do not affect the ranking. The ranking is based solely on how users voted.
